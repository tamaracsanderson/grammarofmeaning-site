kicker: Grammar of Meaning

# What a text is made of

*An **HDS** (Harvard Divinity School) thesis proposal — a theory of how meaning is made, and a method that makes the theory testable. It is offered to religious studies, tested first on scripture and then on ordinary secular texts to show the method is a property of the instrument, not of any one tradition.*

**The problem it answers.** Much close reading of a sacred text treats it as a container: the meaning is inside, and interpretation is the work of drawing it out. That approach handles a single settled reading well, but it leaves the questions the field actually argues about — why two traditions read the same passage in opposite ways, what a text's silences are doing, why one variant matters — resting on the interpreter's judgment, with no shared way to check one reading against another.

**The theory.** A text is not a container; it is a set of **moves** — small, named actions, each recorded in the same few slots: who acts, the operation, what it acts on, its outcome, and who is telling it. What a text *means* depends on what you **recompose** those moves into — including the space *around* them, what they leave unsaid: hold the moves still and turn them a different way, and a different meaning comes into view. The instrument recomposes a text in four ways — the silence-scan runs across everything it has coded; the other three are so far pilot runs on particular texts — and each yields a reading you can contest at a specific move rather than in general:

- **its silences** — what a move raises but never answers; a scan the instrument runs on every text it codes.
- **the alternatives it could have been** — the engine generates the other moves the text could have made in a given spot, keeps only the ones that stay coherent, and reads the actual move as a *choice against* those alternatives. In a pilot on the line "She Loves You," it has done this 36 times: the meaning of the line is what it rules out — not reporting your own feelings, not "she *doesn't* feel for you." The point is not the opposite word; it is that the choice, and its cost, become explicit and reproducible.
- **the readings history gave it** — how later readers recomposed the same moves (a pilot run on Paul's letter to the Romans, chapter 8).
- **its variants across tellings** — the same moment told more than once, differently (the four canonical gospels each narrate the empty tomb, and their differences are data, not error).

Four recompositions, four readings, one text — not ranked, and not always in agreement. Each is a reading you can check at a named move, which is what distinguishes them from a matter of taste.

Two of these four read the text's **negative space** — what it *chose against* (the alternatives) and what it *leaves unsaid* (the silences) — and that negative space is where the instrument does its most distinctive work. Most reading extracts what a text says; this one also reads what it does not, and treats the unsaid as data rather than as absence.

**The method** is what makes those readings checkable, and its core is built, not promised. Every text is read the same way: a **move grammar** that files each move in the same fixed slots — who acts, the operation, what it acts on, its outcome, and who is telling it — recently extended to handle moves with no actor at all, such as a state or a speech-act; **seven coordinates** that place the text (among them: on what grounds it claims to know, what it is for, and what it takes to be real); and **gates** that refuse a reading the evidence will not carry — where the evidence is the text's own moves plus the agreement of independent readers who never see each other's answers. The finished stages run as a reproducible pipeline, one text walked step by step, each step's input, operation, and output named and open to inspection; the recomposition stages beyond the core are so far pilot runs and, in one case, still being wired in. A stranger can re-run what exists and disagree **specifically**, at a named move, instead of in general. What the method *is*, and what it has actually been *run on*, are kept as separate claims on separate pages — the claim should not carry numbers that change every time a job finishes.

**Why it is unusual.** The project's two neighbours each hold half of it. Interpretation theory, in its dominant forms, offers an account of meaning but no shared, replicable protocol for producing a reading; computational text-analysis offers a replicable protocol but no theory anchored to the individual interpretive move. A theory with no method is a claim you can only assent to; a method with no theory of meaning can process a text without ever claiming to have read it. Joining them is the contribution: a claim about how meaning is made that a stranger can reproduce, test, and contest, move by move — including the meaning carried by what a text leaves unsaid, which the container model cannot reach at all.
