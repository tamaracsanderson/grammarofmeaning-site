 How we test this 
 The method is the contribution — so how we know it works is the argument, not housekeeping. Every result is checkable, organized by the question a reader asks: how do you know that? Nothing here is graded on one run's fate: a generative engine is graded on aggregate behavior across runs; a deterministic step, on exact reproduction.

 Do independent coders agree, from the same logic? — reliability 
 Does locked-method × locked-model reproduce a coding? 

 Reliability / R-grades R1 (high) / R2 (medium) / R3 (low) per (passage × dimension) cell — one passage can be R1 on one dimension, R3 on another. Generative steps graded on aggregate rates across runs, deterministic steps per-cell. Ex: Romans 8 — a silence was agreed present (α≈0.59) but not obligated (α≈0.03). 
 IRR family — 5IRR / 3IRR / 3P Whether independent LLM cohorts converge (they never see each other's answers). 5IRR = 5 web-UI cohorts → consensus buckets; 3IRR = 3 local models (cheap); 3P = 3-persona (insider / comparativist / practitioner). They compose (3P×5IRR = 15 reads). Ex: IRR167. Convergence is task-dependent , and agreement ≠ validity — see external review below. 

 Does the agreement mean anything — and does hostile review agree? — external review & agreement ≠ validity 
 Is multi-LLM agreement the same as validity? (No.) And what does an outside adversary find? 

 Agreement is task-dependent — 307 rounds Across 307 IRR reconciliation rounds , agreement is not flat — it tracks task difficulty : models converge when asked to enumerate what exists (~42% of findings 5/5 unanimous) and diverge when asked to code / bound / label a specific unit of text (~25% unanimous, ~29% outright split). That the agreement knows which questions are hard is signal it is not pure shared bias — but it tells us where to trust the instrument (enumeration) versus where a human gold-anchor is required (coding / judgment).  Does it hold beyond its home domain? — instrument vs. domain 
 Is a result a property of the instrument, or of the theology? 

 Out-of-domain replication Religion is the proof-of-concept domain; secular corpora (a pop lyric, a translation set, a book vs. a passage) are confound-free test-beds — a clean test-bed is a floor , not a difficulty proxy. Ex: the Romans-8 present-vs-obligated split reproduced on a Beatles pop lyric (IRR167, 5/5), turning a suspected theology problem into a demonstrated instrument problem. Replication failures are findings, not embarrassments. 

 Does it over-generate — a negative control — the first test with a RESULT 
 Give any instrument four layers and a dozen loci and it can always find plausible “gaps.” So we ran the opposite test. 

 ✓ RUN Negative control — the MIT License A text engineered to close interpretive space (legal boilerplate — the opposite of scripture). A well-calibrated instrument should find almost nothing; a generator would find twenty. Result: 12 candidates → ~81% discarded by the eligibility gates; 2 clean survivors — and they are the license’s two genuinely famous silences ( patent-rights scope ; the undefined “substantial portions” threshold). Every discard is named. The rule it crystallizes: a gap is not anything the text doesn’t say — it is something the text’s own words open but don’t close. The control passes. (First control run; the diverse-corpus battery is the confirming scale-up.) The full probe →

 Does perturbing a condition change the reading? — RESITUATE, the dynamic engine 
 The inverse of the static gap-scan: change one load-bearing condition, watch the reading swing, keep only the seams. 

 ✓ RUN RESITUATE — 5-text demo Perturb a text’s load-bearing conditions → watch the reading swing → a two-stage filter keeps only the seams (absent / implied-and-activated) and discards everything stated . Run clean on 5 texts across narrative / lyric / apophatic / aphoristic / argument (Jonah · Dickinson · Tao Te Ching · Dhammapada · 1 Corinthians 15). Findings: the filter discards the stated conditions everywhere; translation-seams dominate the non-English texts (a translator’s forced choice the reading leans on); and RESITUATE catches two seams no other engine gets — a boundary-artifact seam (where the passage is cut) and an edition seam (which text you hold).  Two halves of one instrument: the static move-scan finds in-text silences; RESITUATE (dynamic) finds the seams between the text and its context or version. Neither is the other — together they are the candidate-engine union. 

 Does the method improve from its own tests? — the living instrument 
 Not a static pipeline — a living object that improves from its own test feedback, and pools its engines through one gate. 

 Iterate from test feedback Every test (A/B, the reflexive / IRR224 adversarial reviews, future panels) feeds a backlog of candidate improvements; a candidate graduates by recurrence , not one run’s whim.  Seeded critics, not role-played A crit panel is only as good as what its critics are seeded on — real fetched text, not the model’s impression of a name. Proof: a bare-vs-seeded A/B on William James .  Is the data + spec sound, and actually built? — audit 
 Does the code match the claim, and the data match the schema? 

 Internal audits Data integrity + spec-vs-reality drift, run nightly / pre-commit / per-sprint: db-hygiene, state-audit, the spec-vs-implementation gap audit (specs with no code behind them), the nightly doc-drift auditor . Dashboards read the DB, not narration — they can't be talked past. 
 XT — programmatic tests An empirical question run at $0, producing data (vs. an IRR, which produces judgments ). Ex: XT1 cross-model persona ( frame dominates, not model ), XT4 move-segmentation reliability. 

 Does an independent re-derivation reproduce it? — clean-room 
 Can someone else get the same result, without our framing? 

 Clean-room review A finished artifact checked by an independent party who does not see the author's framing — catches contamination and bias. It answers is what we wrote true? 
 Clean-room implementation Whether the method reproduces from its written spec alone , no tacit knowledge — an independent coder builds from the logic, outputs compared. The "logic, then code from it" test; its value is that it proves the method is not idiosyncratic — someone else can run it. 

 Two worked cases, on separate pages — they test different things, and both are long enough to read on their own: 

 The data-dictionary audit — a clean-room review . Three blind lenses against our own field definitions; ~40 problems in 129 fields, two of them introduced that same evening. 
 The engine reproduction — a clean-room implementation . Whether Engines 0–2 rebuild from the written spec alone. They reproduce through Engine 0 and stop at Engine 1. 

 The pairing is the point: the first checks whether our description is true, the second whether the method is reproducible by someone who isn't us. An artifact can pass the first and fail the second. 

 How do we pick a build when several designs are valid? — clean-room triangulation 
 When the approach is genuinely uncertain, don’t anchor on the first idea — get independent ones, then reconcile. 

 Clean-room triangulation (CRT) N independent parties (usually three), no contact , each handed the same design brief — whose outputs we reconcile into a hybrid . The independence is the whole value: divergence is the signal , and a lone reviewer ships plausible-but-wrong. It is the build/design sibling of the reliability testing above — reliability asks do independent coders agree? (converge); CRT asks how would independent builders do this? (diverge for coverage, then synthesize). Distinct from a 5-LLM IRR, which drives a methodology-truth question toward consensus. Proven across three S157 builds — a lineage-detector, an engineering-speed review, and a country-scale church map. The CRT playbook — brief anatomy, the 5 steps, the CRT-vs-IRR distinction → 

 Sibling to the reliability numbers at the top: those ask whether independent coders converge on the same reading; CRT deliberately makes independent builders diverge , because on an open design the disagreement is the coverage. 

 Does the generated map match reality? — predictive 
 Do the outputs predict what actually happened? 

 Round-trip / predictive validity Reverse-coding (code → back to text) + whether Engine 3's possible answer-space predicts Engine 4's attested reception. The generated field is the controlled variable; the attested record is the observed one. (Designed — Engines 3/4 not yet built.) 

 Could this check have failed? — discrimination 
 Guards against silent fallback — could this check have failed? 

 Discriminating-check rule Guards against silent fallback — a step fails, the system substitutes something plausible, and the output still looks normal. The guard is one question asked of every check: could this have failed? A verification whose marker lives in the data being displayed rather than in the code that changed will pass against the old build and the new one alike. Fires whenever a fix is verified. Instances caught in a single session (2026-07-23): a constraint violation that made the pipeline fall back to a stale tree; a git stash that swallowed untracked work; a label truncated mid-word by a silent [:34] ; a scheduled job installed but never loaded, so it had never once run; a 0-byte database file that answered "no such table" instead of "this file is empty." Each degraded quietly and kept producing plausible output. 

 Sibling to ghost-protection below: that one catches fabricated claims; this one catches unfalsifiable checks and quiet degradation . 

 Is a claimed past decision real? — ghost-protection 
 Guarding against fabricated provenance. 

 Citation-or-don't-claim + adversarial spot-checks Any "we decided X at session N" must cite a specific Decision / Proposal / journal entry, or it is flagged unverified. Adversarial verifiers are prompted to refute , not confirm. The dashboards above are the primary deterministic ghost-detector. 

 Why this is a contribution, not a checklist. Most comparative work publishes findings and withholds the apparatus. This project publishes the apparatus — so the checks are the open-methodology claim. Its honest counterpart is on the schema itself: the ⚠ undefined counts. Showing the gaps and showing the test suite are the same commitment. 

 Can it find real research gaps? — the R&D application 
 Run the gap-finder on a foundational research paper — do the gaps it surfaces hold up? 

 ✓ RUN Generative Agents (Park et al., “Smallville”) We ran the instrument on arXiv 2304.03442 — the generative-agents paper — as a research gap-finder, reasoning only from the paper’s own words (no hindsight, no later literature). It surfaced genuine, defensible gaps, each anchored to an activating phrase, each passing the eligibility gates (nitpicks + in-window-resolved items rejected). Top three: (G1) a metric-validity confound — the headline believability effect (d=8.16) is a rated-appearance delta, and the paper’s own limitations name surface confounds (“overly cooperative,” “embellishment”) the metric can’t separate from architectural merit; (G2) scope / timescale — planning is claimed for a “longer time horizon” but evidenced on a “short timescale” by the paper’s own admission; (G7) a floor-effect — the ~8 SD is measured against a baseline “without access to anything.” A 4-persona crit panel (ML-systems / cognitive-scientist / adversarial-skeptic / HCI-product) then disagreed on which gap matters most — that divergence is the signal, not a consensus. All 8 load-bearing quotes verified against the published PDF with page citations.
