# The engine (one line)
Perturb a Sitz/Frame condition → does the reading SWING? → if yes, is the condition STATED (discard: load-bearing-but-said) or
ABSENT/IMPLIED-and-activated (keep: a SEAM)? A swing alone is never a gap.

# Cross-text findings (the demo's payoff)
1. **The two-stage filter works everywhere** — every run discarded the STATED conditions (Dickinson's grave, Dhammapada's
   similes, 1 Cor's "Christ was raised", TTC's surface paradox) and kept only absent/implied-and-activated seams. The `stated
   ⇒ discard` control fired cleanly on all 5.
2. **Translation-seams are the dominant class on the non-English texts** (TTC 4/6, Dhammapada 3/5): the seam is *a translator's
   committed choice standing in for erased source-language indeterminacy* — invisible to the English reader precisely because a
   word covers it. This is Compare-feeding-gaps-across-languages, made concrete → the multilingual capability is real.
3. **RESITUATE surfaces SEAMS, not GAPS** — every kept item is an unsaid *between* the text and its context/version, not an
   in-text silence. Empirical validation of the S156 gap/seam naming lock.
4. **Two RESITUATE-only catches no other engine gets:** the *boundary-artifact* seam (1 Cor S4 — an artifact of where the
   container is cut) and the *edition* seam (Dickinson — which text you hold). Both are conditions the reading leans on that a
   move-scan or situate pass, reading one fixed version, structurally cannot see.
