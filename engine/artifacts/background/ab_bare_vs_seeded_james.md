# The test (PI's idea)
Same critic (William James), same gap (Smallville G1 — believability = rated appearance), two ways: (A) "William James" from
model memory; (B) James SEEDED with his actual text — *Varieties of Religious Experience*, Lecture III "The Reality of the
Unseen" (fetched: Gutenberg #621 primary, corroborated at authorama/CCEL; §13 caveat: re-verify middle quotes vs a paginated
critical edition before thesis use — Gutenberg pull truncated mid-lecture).

# The result — the seed rerouted the critique
- **Bare-James (memory):** "believability is *merely* subjective, so discount it." (The regress-to-the-mean of the name:
  James → pragmatism → 'it's subjective.')
- **Seeded-James (his text):** the OPPOSITE, and sharper. James treats the *sense of reality* as a **real, primary faculty**
  ("a feeling of objective presence… 'something there,' more deep and more general than any of the special senses"). So a
  rater's felt sense the agents are alive is *legitimate data*, not noise — which **overrules bare-James's "merely."** Then
  the blade: his hallucination case shows the faculty fires HARDEST where nothing is present ("real in the most emphatic
  sense of the word… neither seen, heard, touched"). Applied to G1: believability measures a real faculty that is
  **decoupled from actual presence/correctness** — so a "facade of realism" scores as high as real merit, and the paper's
  admitted confounds ("overly cooperative," "embellishment") are exactly the stimuli that excite it. The metric is
  **worse, not softer.** Plus self-sealing: James — "your critical arguments, be they never so superior, will vainly set
  themselves to change his faith" — so the paper's after-the-fact confound disclaimers can't rescue it.

# Why it matters (the FAQ answer)
The **diff between Arm A and Arm B is the evidence that seeding works.** A bare named-persona regresses to the mean of the
name (a prior-driven guess); a seeded critic is *constrained and sometimes corrected* by a specific, auditable document —
and here the correction reversed the conclusion (memory said "discount"; the text said "the effect is real and THAT's the
problem"). Same shape as the Gelman overrule. **So: our critics are NOT generic act-like-Socrates personas — a seeded critic
≠ the thinker's vibe, it's the thinker's text, and the difference is visible + auditable.** Unseeded named-personas should be
treated as prior-driven guesses, flagged (§13), never presented as the thinker.
