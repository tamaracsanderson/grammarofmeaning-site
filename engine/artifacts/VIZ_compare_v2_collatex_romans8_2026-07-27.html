What comes out: the drift is now token-exact and witness-neutral — you can see where the three renderings hold the same word and where each chose differently (e.g. work-together vs. work, foreknew vs. fore-appoint), against a spine no single translation owns. Reuse, not rebuild: CollateX is the maintained reference implementation (GPL-3.0; the reuse research flagged its Python port is pinned to a 2023 release — pinned here). Honest limits (two): (1) a gap can be a word-order artifact — KJV renders "to them that love God" earlier, so CollateX aligns it to a different column and gaps here; not an omission. (2) Collation shows where they differ, not why — "we have known" (YLT) vs "we know" (KJV/DRA) is the Greek perfect οἴδαμεν; that tense/aspect reason lives in the coding/Greek-source layer, not here. All three witnesses are ENGLISH (Latin Vulgate, Greek SBLGNT, German Luther-1545 are on disk but not in this view). Data: real PD editions in field_ed/romans8_editions + ylt_romans.json. S156 v2.
