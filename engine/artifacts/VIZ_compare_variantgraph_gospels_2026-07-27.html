The resurrection, as a spine with branches

Four gospel resurrection accounts collated by move (not raw words) through CollateX's Gothenburg variant-graph model. Where all four narrate the same beat, the paths merge into one spine node. Where a gospel drops a beat or inserts its own, the path branches. The spine below is computed by CollateX; the branch nodes are the coded signature moves quoted from the audit.

The shared spine

10 beats are carried by all four gospels (solid nodes); 6 by three, 4 by two (dashed = partial spine). The lower strip shows, per beat, which gospels carry it — a filled dot = carries, a struck dot = omits. Read left → right; hover any node for the full coded beat + note.

carried by all 4 (spine) carried by 2–3 (partial / divergence) · struck dot in the strip = that gospel omits the beat

All four accounts converge on a first-day dawn visit to the tomb by female follower(s), a stone found displaced, one or more white/dazzling figures present, an announcement or discovery that Jesus's body is absent, and a departure to carry word back to the male disciples. The synoptics share a tighter verbal spine — an explicit 'he is not here / he has been raised' formula paired with a commission and appeal to Jesus's prior words — while JOHN substitutes an empirical inspection of the linen cloths and a scripture-frame for the same beats. Around this shared skeleton the accounts vary chiefly in the number and location of the numinous figures, the witnesses' terminal affect (fear, joy, amazement, belief), and whether male disciples subsequently verify the scene.

Where each gospel branches off

Every account keeps the spine but hangs its own tributaries on it. Each colored node below is a coded move unique to that gospel — the insertions CollateX marks as single-witness branches. Open the drawers for what each gospel drops or reworks.

Matthew 28

7 adds · 4 omits · 6 reworks

MATT-28 stages the resurrection as a public, apocalyptic theophany — earthquake, descending angel, enthroned upon the broken seal, with imperial guards struck down as collateral witnesses — turning the shared empty-tomb spine into a cosmic enthronement scene whose commissioned messengers depart in fear fused with joy toward a promised Galilean reunion.

BRANCHES — coded moves unique to this gospel
Omits from the shared spine (4)
  • male disciple(s) go to the tomb to verify the women's report (partial spine beat: LUKE/JOHN) — MATT has no verification visit
  • the linen burial cloths seen lying in the tomb (partial spine beat: LUKE/JOHN)
  • the announcement being routed specifically to Peter within the disciple circle (spine beat carried by MARK/LUKE/JOHN) — MATT names 'his disciples' generically
  • a closing state left unresolved into full belief (partial spine beat: MARK/LUKE) — MATT resolves toward commissioned joy
Reworks a shared beat (6)
  • stone-displacement (spine): reshaped from divine-passive discovery into an on-stage angelic act with a named agent and audience
  • numinous figure(s) present (spine): singular angel positioned OUTSIDE atop the stone, not inside the tomb
  • witnesses' fear-reaction (spine): split across two subjects — terror falls on the guards, while the women's fear is folded into joy
  • invitation to view the burial spot (spine): rendered as a direct imperative pair 'come / see where he lay' rather than as empirical discovery
  • prior word of Jesus invoked (spine): pointed forward to a Galilean reunion rather than backward to a passion-prediction
  • departure and report (spine): intensified into an urgent commanded run, with the message's angelic provenance explicitly stamped

Mark 16

4 adds · 2 omits · 5 reworks

MARK-16 tells the resurrection as a scene of terrified aphasia — a muted young man's announcement met by women who flee, tremble, and fail to speak — leaving the empty-tomb spine hanging on an unfulfilled commission that only a later scribal hand dares to seal shut with a re-commissioning and liturgical amen.

BRANCHES — coded moves unique to this gospel
Omits from the shared spine (2)
  • male disciple(s) go to the tomb to verify the women's report (partial spine beat: LUKE/JOHN)
  • the linen burial cloths seen lying in the tomb (partial spine beat: LUKE/JOHN)
Reworks a shared beat (5)
  • witnesses depart the tomb (spine): reshaped from an ordinary departure into flight gripped by terror and amazement as personified affects that seize them
  • witnesses commissioned to report (spine): the commission is issued but explicitly UNFULFILLED at the original ending, then reinstated in the long-ending as a divine-passive re-order
  • numinous figure at the tomb (spine): a 'young man' rather than a named angel, seated inside on the right — deliberately understated
  • prior word of Jesus invoked (spine): cited as generic 'as he told you' with the content elided, not spelled out
  • closing affective state (spine-partial): sharpened into fearful silence — the account originally ends on unresolved dread with no proclamation

Luke 24

6 adds · 1 omits · 6 reworks

LUKE-24 tells the resurrection as an exercise in RE-COGNITION — the women discover the absence themselves, are enjoined to remember a past passion-prediction rather than await a future Galilean reunion, and the announcement is received as an idle tale by apostles whose disbelief (and Peter's unresolved amazement) is itself part of the story.

BRANCHES — coded moves unique to this gospel
Omits from the shared spine (1)
  • a promise of future seeing/reunion with the risen Jesus in Galilee (partial spine beat: MATT/MARK) — LUKE relocates Galilee to the past-remembering, not to a future rendezvous
Reworks a shared beat (6)
  • body-absence discovered (spine): reshaped from angelic declaration to empirical find-not by the women BEFORE any figure appears — discovery precedes announcement
  • invitation to view the vacated spot (spine): collapsed into the women's own prior finding rather than a directive to 'come, see'
  • prior word of Jesus invoked (spine): reframed as an imperative to REMEMBER his past passion-prediction, with the content quoted in full, rather than pointing to a Galilean promise
  • witnesses commissioned to report (spine): no explicit commission — the women self-initiate the telling on their own remembered authority
  • male disciple(s) verify the tomb (partial spine beat): carried but reshaped as Peter running solo, seeing only the linens, and departing amazed — verification yields wonder, not belief
  • crucified-identity of the sought one (spine): folded inside the divine-necessity 'Son of Man' prediction rather than named as an angelic identifying mark

John 20

9 adds · 5 omits · 6 reworks

JOHN-20 tells the resurrection as an interpretive drama of misreading and dawning faith — Mary reads the empty tomb as a theft, the disciples race to inspect the linens as forensic evidence, angels pastorally interrogate rather than proclaim, and the empty-tomb spine culminates not in announcement but in one disciple's silent seeing-and-believing while scripture waits to be understood.

BRANCHES — coded moves unique to this gospel
Omits from the shared spine (5)
  • the sought one is identified as the crucified Jesus (spine) — crucifixion is not verbalized in this tomb scene
  • his absence from the tomb is explicitly declared 'he is not here' (spine) — no formulaic declaration
  • his resurrected/raised status is announced by the numinous figures (spine) — the angels only ask about weeping; resurrection is left to inference and to a narrator-aside
  • a promise of future seeing/reunion with the risen Jesus in Galilee (partial spine) — no future-reunion promise issued
  • a closing unresolved cognitive/affective state (partial spine) — JOHN resolves the beloved disciple into belief
Reworks a shared beat (6)
  • female follower at the tomb (spine): shrunk from a group to Mary Magdalene alone
  • figures address the witnesses with an interpreting speech-act (spine): reshaped from proclamation/commission to a bare pastoral question ('woman, why are you weeping?')
  • witnesses commissioned to report (spine): recast as Mary's own unsolicited theft-report to Peter and the beloved disciple, not an angelic commission
  • male disciples verify the tomb (partial spine): elevated from a marginal add-on to a central set-piece with staged roles (outrunner, enterer, believer)
  • invitation to view the vacated spot (spine): reshaped into empirical inspection of the burial linens rather than a directive 'come, see'
  • prior word invoked (spine): displaced from a saying of Jesus to a general 'scripture' the disciples do not yet understand
