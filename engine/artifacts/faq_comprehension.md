# Comprehension

Questions people ask to *understand* the method — "what is this / how does it work?" — as distinct from the objections ("isn't this a hole?") further down.

## What is the morphospace, and how does MCA work?

Every reading is coded on 8 categorical axes — its **frame profile**. To *see* which readings resemble each other, we need a map where similar profiles sit near each other.

**MCA (Multiple Correspondence Analysis) builds that map.** It's the categorical cousin of **PCA**: PCA is for *numbers* (heights, weights); MCA is for *categories* (our axis-values). We use MCA, and it does the whole job in one step — it looks at all 911 profiles, finds the *combinations of values that most separate* the readings, and turns each reading into coordinates (x, y, z, …). *(PCA is not a separate step — it's just the more-familiar name for the same idea on numeric data. MCA is both the analysis and the coordinates.)*

MCA produces **many ranked dimensions**, each capturing a slice of the total structure ("inertia"), biggest first. The map shows the top 2 (**41.9%**).

**The intuition — flattening a globe onto a paper map:** you keep the big shapes and lose some detail; a 3rd dimension (the globe itself) would recover more, but you can't see it all at once on a flat page. So the 2D map is an **orientation device**: *near = similar frame-profiles*, the two drawn axes are the two biggest **composite contrasts** (not 2 of the 8 coded axes), and the ~58% not shown lives in the dimensions you're not looking at.

**The four corners are emergent, not imposed.** The typology at the map's corners (source × posture → *descriptive-integrative*, *critical-materialist*, *devotional-sacramental*, *prophetic-transformative*) is **not a grid we drew** — the geometry found it, and it maps onto real tradition-families unprompted.

## When do you stop adding dimensions? (the "80/20" question)

Two shorthands: the **elbow (scree) rule** — stop where the added slice drops off (ours drops hard after dim 1, then flattens → 2-3D for the map); and the **average-inertia rule** — keep dimensions carrying more than their fair share. By that rule **51 dimensions survive here** — so there's **no clean 80/20: meaning-space is genuinely high-dimensional.** You stop at the elbow (2-3D) for the *map*, knowing it's an orientation device — and *that the tail is long is itself a finding*: meaning-making doesn't collapse to a few axes.
