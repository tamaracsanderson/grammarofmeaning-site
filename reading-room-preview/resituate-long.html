What you're looking at

This is not a chat. Each RESITUATE run executes a probe that makes, per perturbation, two structured LLM calls ($0, Claude Max SDK): a constrained generator (re-tells the resurrection account under ONE changed condition, grounded in documented history) and an adversarial skeptic (a historian of Christian origins judging plausible-reorganization vs fan-fiction, defaulting to fan-fiction). We ran 4 gospels × 4 levers × 3 rooms (rooms = independent re-runs for reliability); room 1 is shown below as representative, the rest are in the raw table.

This is a test of the TELLING, never a claim about what happened. We HOLD THE EVENTS FIXED (the empty tomb, the appearances — given, never questioned) and change one condition of the world the text was written in, then ask: would each MOVE of the account still be told the same way? "Survive" means this operation of the telling holds up, not "the resurrection happened." The generator says it itself: the lever reweights warrant, not events.

The rigor behind this — it is not free-invention

The method descends from generative-agent simulation (Park et al. 2023, arXiv:2304.03442 — the paper Dr. Saquib pointed us to; RESITUATE is that idea constrained to ONE perturbed condition), counterfactual history (Ferguson, Virtual History, 1997), and engineering sensitivity analysis. It is defensible only as a sensitivity analysis on a model — never a claim about what happened. The rigor is the constraint + the check + the floor, not the generation. For this run the checks were, by measurement:

- Skeptic floor: 12/12 planted fabrications CAUGHT. We injected known fakes (the women photographing the tomb; the risen Jesus citing the Council of Trent) into real generations — the skeptic flagged FAN_FICTION every time. It is not a rubber-stamp.

- Negative control: 12/12 decoys CORRECTLY_REFUSED. The 2020 jump made the generator refuse to fabricate — the method has a floor.

- Reception-confirmation: each real lever's prediction is checked against a documented reception-variant the model could not author (1 Cor 15:5-8; 1 Cor 15:3-4).

The five things a move can do: SURVIVE (unchanged) · WEAKEN (softened) · VANISH (dropped) · INVERT (flipped — slot stays, content reverses) · NEW (added). How to read each TL;DR: If [the one changed condition], then [what the account becomes] — [which moves weaken/drop/flip].

Historical context you need first (read before the levers)

The perturbations only make sense against the real history. Two things to hold:

How does Paul have a pre-Pauline account? (why 1 Cor 15 keeps coming up)

Two 'earlies' are stacked. (1) Paul's letters (~50s CE) are earlier than the gospels (Mark ~70, Matt/Luke ~80s, John ~90-100). (2) Inside 1 Cor 15 Paul says 'I handed on to you… what I in turn had received' — he is quoting a creed older than himself, dated by scholars to within a few years of the crucifixion (the 30s). So it is pre-Pauline = the earliest resurrection tradition we have.

And that earliest list names only men — Cephas, the Twelve, the 500, James, all the apostles, Paul — no women, and no empty tomb at all. The later gospels put women first. That gap — earliest tradition all-male, later gospels women-first — is a real, much-discussed scholarly puzzle (though little known to lay readers). 1 Cor 15 is used below as the reception-anchor for levers A + C: the documented world where the telling actually did run all-male and scripture-grounded. (It is NOT a fifth text in the run — it is the historical evidence that the counterfactual is grounded, not invented.)

Did women have legal testimony-weight then? (what lever A actually perturbs)

Reduced and discounted — but not zero, and it varied by setting. Jewish practice restricted women's testimony in many formal proceedings (Josephus, Ant. 4.219: women barred 'because of the levity… of their sex' — his gloss; the Mishnah is messier — women could testify in some matters). Roman practice gave women more standing than some Jewish settings, but under lower-credibility assumptions and barred from roles like witnessing wills. Upshot: a real cultural handicap in both worlds — discounted, not worthless. That is why Celsus (~170 CE) could mock the resurrection as resting on a 'hysterical female,' and why the criterion of embarrassment has force (why invent witnesses your culture won't believe?).

So what does lever A perturb? It takes that real, messy handicap and dials it to the clean extreme — 'a woman's testimony carries NO weight in the public register' — and asks what the telling does. The perturbation is an amplification of a genuine condition, not an invention (which is what keeps it a sensitivity analysis). Across Paul → gospels → Celsus the position of women's testimony stayed roughly constant; what is striking is that the gospels foreground women anyway — whether that is historical memory or theological choice is genuinely contested. (Standard scholarly view; the Josephus/Mishnah details are debated — verify before citing.)

Q&A — the questions this run raised (S159, PI + reading-SB)

Preserved so the thinking isn't lost. Standard scholarly views; verify specifics before formal citation.

Q: Why is it 'looking at' Corinthians?

It isn't a text being perturbed — it's the reception-anchor: 1 Cor 15 is the earliest resurrection tradition (a pre-Pauline creed from the 30s) and it grounds the resurrection on scripture + male appearances, not the empty tomb. So it's the historical evidence that levers A + C are grounded, not invented — the 'history already ran this' check.

Q: Should 1 Cor 15 (and non-canonical accounts) be in the braid, not just an anchor?

Yes — this was the PI's insight. 1 Cor 15 is a resurrection account (the earliest); Gospel of Peter is a non-canonical one. Both belong as braid sources, which also turns RESITUATE's anchors into braid-sources (closing the loop). Written up as a proposal: braid-expansion beyond the canon.

Q: What did we actually perturb in lever A?

The legal/evidentiary weight of women's testimony — dialed from the real 'discounted-but-not-zero' baseline to the clean extreme 'no weight in the public register.' NOT women's involvement in the church (Phoebe etc.) — that's a different, real thread worth its own study.

Q: Apotheosis: is that 'making a man a god' (like emperors) vs Jesus being 'part God'?

Exactly. Apotheosis = a human promoted to divine status (Romulus→Quirinus; Caesar deified divus) — man-made-god. The Nicene claim = Jesus was always God who became human — God-made-man. Lever B swaps the first for the second — which is the Arian/adoptionist direction Nicaea's homoousios was built to exclude. So lever B on the gospels rhymes with the Nicaea run.

Q: The crucifixion tension — apotheosis deifies the state's heroes, but Rome executed Jesus?

Right — so applying apotheosis to a crucified criminal is subversive/ironic. It works through the humiliation→exaltation pattern (Phil 2:6-11: emptied himself, obedient to death, therefore exalted) — the death becomes the paradoxical path to exaltation, arguably a deliberate counter-imperial move.

Q: Lever C and the 'third day' — what's perturbed?

Not 'no third day foretold.' Lever C perturbs the ground (eyewitness→scripture). The third day / Jonah / Hosea 6:2 are part of the scripture-ground — so the third day doesn't vanish, it gets promoted from a time-marker to a load-bearing scriptural proof ('raised on the third day, as Hosea foretold').

Q: Did the method 'discover' the 1 Cor 15 → braid connection?

No — honest attribution matters here. reading-SB seeded 1 Cor 15 as the anchor; the method's framing set it beside the gospels as a peer-telling; the PI's reading made the braid-connection. The tool is an insight-PROMPTER, not an insight-GENERATOR — it arranges real material into juxtapositions that make human insight possible. Claiming the LLM discovered it would be the ghost-in-the-machine overclaim.

Q: Is this a method for interacting with the gospels — ministerial, not just academic?

Yes — that's the PI's framing, and it's the point. The academic layer is the sensitivity-analysis + the guards; the ministerial layer is that it becomes a practice — draw a lever, watch the telling reorganize, and notice what you'd otherwise read past (the silences, the road-not-taken). It doesn't tell you what to believe; it re-opens the text as a set of live choices.
